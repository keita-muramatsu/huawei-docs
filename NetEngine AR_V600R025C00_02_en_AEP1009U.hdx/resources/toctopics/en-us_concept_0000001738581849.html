Understanding POS Interfaces

Introduction to SONET and SDH

The SONET standard was defined by the American National Standards Institute (ANSI) for synchronous digital transmission. It is mainly used in North America and Japan. In terms of synchronous transmission, SONET defines hierarchical line rates for the optical transmission system, with 51.84 Mbit/s as the baseline. This baseline rate is called optical carrier level 1 (OC-1).

SONET-based SDH is an international standard defined by the International Telecommunication Union Telecommunication Standardization Sector (ITU-T) and mainly used in Europe. SDH and SONET are essentially the same except the baseline rate in SDH is 155.52 Mbit/s. This rate is called synchronous transport module level 1 (STM-1) and is equivalent to optical carrier level 3 (OC-3) in SONET.

For the transmission rates supported by the device, see Get to Know the Product > Hardware Description > Cards > POS Card.

Introduction to POS

POS applies to MANs and WANs. It encapsulates IP packets into Point-to-Point Protocol (PPP) or High-Level Data Link Control (HDLC) packets, maps the encapsulated packets to the payload of SONET/SDH signals by using the service adapter at the SONET/SDH channel layer, puts the payload into a SONET/SDH frame after adding the channel overhead at the transport layer and the section overhead at the section layer, and finally sends the frame to the physical layer (optical fiber) of the optical network for transmission.
