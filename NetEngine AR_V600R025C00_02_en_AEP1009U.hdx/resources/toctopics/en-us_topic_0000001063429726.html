Assembling the Shielded RJ45 Connector and Ethernet Cable

Context

Figure Shielded RJ45 connector and cable shows the components of an RJ45 connector and a shielded Ethernet cable.

Figure 1 Shielded RJ45 connector and cable

A. Jacket of connector

B. Metal shell of connector

C. Wire holder of connector

D. Plug of connector

E. Jacket of Ethernet cable

F. Shield layer of Ethernet cable

G. Twisted-pair wires

-

Procedure

  1. Fit the jacket of the connector onto the Ethernet cable, as shown in Figure Fitting the jacket of the connector onto the Ethernet cable.

    Figure 2 Fitting the jacket of the connector onto the Ethernet cable

  2. Remove a 30 mm (1.18 in.) long section of the jacket, cut off the nylon twine inside the jacket, and cut a no more than 5 mm (0.20 in.) cleft in the jacket, as shown in Figure Removing the jacket of a twisted-pair cable (unit: mm (in.)).

    • When you remove a section of the jacket, do not damage the shield layer of the twisted-pair cable.
    • When you remove the shield layer, do not damage the insulation of the twisted-pair cable.
    Figure 3 Removing the jacket of a twisted-pair cable (unit: mm (in.))

  3. Fit the metal shell onto the twisted-pair cable. The shield layer is covered by the metal shell, as shown in Figure Fitting the metal shell onto the twisted-pair cable.

    Figure 4 Fitting the metal shell onto the twisted-pair cable

  4. Fit the metal shell onto the twisted-pair cable until the shield layer is covered completely. Along the edge of the metal shell, cut off the aluminum foil shield layer and ensure that there is no surplus copper wire. The exposed twisted-pair cable is about 20 mm (0.79 in.) long, as shown in Figure Removing the shield layer of a twisted-pair cable (unit: mm (in.)).

    Figure 5 Removing the shield layer of a twisted-pair cable (unit: mm (in.))

  5. Lead the four pairs of twisted-pair wires through the wire holder, as shown in Figure Leading wires through the wire holder and Figure Cable locations in a wire holder. Ensure that the colored wires are in the correct location in the cable.

    Figure 6 Leading wires through the wire holder
    Figure 7 Cable locations in a wire holder

  6. Align the four pairs of cables in the holder, as shown in Figure Four pairs of cables on a wire holder. The connections between the wires and the pins are shown in Figure Connections between wires and pins and listed in Table Connections between wires and pins (using a straight-through cable as an example).

    Figure 8 Four pairs of cables on a wire holder
    Figure 9 Connections between wires and pins

  7. Cut off the surplus cables along the lower edge of the wire holder, as shown in Figure Cutting off surplus cables.

    Figure 10 Cutting off surplus cables

  8. Put the connector body onto the wire holder and turn the metal shell by 90°, as shown in Figure Putting the connector body onto the wire holder.

    Ensure that the wire holder is in good contact with the connector body.

    Figure 11 Putting the connector body onto the wire holder

  9. Push the metal shell toward the connector body until the wire holder and the connector body are engaged completely. Crimp the connector, as shown in Figure Crimping the connector.

    Figure 12 Crimping the connector

  10. Push the jacket towards the metal shell until the metal shell is covered. This completes the assembly of one end of the cable, as shown in Figure Pushing the metal shell.

    Figure 13 Pushing the metal shell

  11. To complete the assembly of the other end, repeat steps 1 to 10.
