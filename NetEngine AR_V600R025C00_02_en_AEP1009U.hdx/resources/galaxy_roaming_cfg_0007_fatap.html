Overview of Roaming Technology

Roaming technologies are constantly evolving to enhance user experience on WLANs. Initial intra-WAC roaming has evolved into fast roaming using PMK caching, 802.11r fast roaming, AI roaming, and smart roaming. Each generation of roaming is aimed at reducing handover time, improving the roaming sensitivity and success rate, and strengthening connection stability for STAs on the move.

  1. Intra-WAC roaming

    The most basic roaming modes used on early WLANs include intra-WAC roaming.

  2. Fast roaming

    Fast roaming is designed to reduce the roaming handover time of STAs and improve roaming efficiency. Fast roaming technologies include fast roaming using PMK caching and 802.11r fast roaming.

    • Fast roaming using PMK caching: PMK information is cached in advance so that STAs do not need to perform 802.1X authentication again during roaming. Only key negotiation is required after roaming.
    • 802.11r fast roaming: The Fast Basic Service Set Transition (FT) function defined in 802.11r further optimizes the authentication and key negotiation procedures during roaming.
  3. Smart roaming

    Smart roaming analyzes STA information such as the signal strength and access rate, and automatically selects the most appropriate AP as the roaming target, improving the roaming success rate and user experience. Smart roaming works with load balancing algorithms to ensure proper network resource allocation.

  4. AI roaming

    AI roaming is an enhancement of smart roaming technology. A WAC provides personalized steering policies for different types of STAs and starts steering at a proper time through the coordinated measurement mechanism. This improves the roaming sensitivity and success rate.
