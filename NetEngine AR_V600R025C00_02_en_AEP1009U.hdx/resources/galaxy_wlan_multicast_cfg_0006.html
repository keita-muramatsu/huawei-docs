Understanding IGMP Snooping of APs

IGMP snooping is a basic Layer 2 multicast function that forwards and controls multicast traffic at the data link layer. After this function is enabled, an AP listens to and analyzes IGMP messages transmitted between the upper-layer Layer 3 device and STAs, and creates and maintains multicast forwarding entries to instruct multicast data forwarding only in VAPs that has multicast members, thereby reducing unnecessary wireless data transmission.

As shown in Figure 1, on a WLAN with multicast deployed, VAP1 connects to STA1 and STA2, VAP2 connects to STA3, and only STA1 is a multicast member. Before IGMP snooping is enabled on the AP, multicast data is broadcast to all STAs over the air interface. After IGMP snooping is enabled on the AP, multicast data is broadcast only in VAP1 that has multicast members.

Suppression of IGMP Report and Leave Messages on APs

IGMP periodically sends query and response messages to maintain group memberships. Members send Report messages in the following scenarios:

  • When joining a multicast group, a member sends a Report message.
  • When receiving an IGMP General Query message, a member sends a Report message.
  • A member running IGMPv2 or IGMPv3 sends a Leave message when leaving a multicast group.

During this process, if multiple members of a multicast group exist on the AP, the AP may repeatedly forward the same Report or Leave message to the upstream device.

Suppression of IGMP Report and Leave messages on APs can reduce the number of packets on the network. After this function is configured, the AP forwards only one IGMP Membership Report message to the upstream device.

  • When the first member joins a multicast group or a member sends a Report message in response to an IGMP Query message, the AP forwards a Report message to the upstream device.
  • When the last member of a multicast group leaves the group, the AP forwards a Leave message to the upstream device.
