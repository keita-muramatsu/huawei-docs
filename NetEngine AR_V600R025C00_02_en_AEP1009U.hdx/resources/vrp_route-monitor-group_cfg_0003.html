Understanding Route Monitoring Groups

Each route in a route monitoring group has a down-weight. A service module can be associated with a route monitoring group to perform link switching based on the down-weight sum of the route monitoring group. The implementation is as follows:

  • A route monitoring group monitors the status of its member routes. If a route in the group becomes inactive, its down-weight is added to the down-weight sum of the route monitoring group. When N routes become inactive, the sum of their down-weights is the down-weight sum of the route monitoring group. When the down-weight sum of the route monitoring group reaches or exceeds the switchover threshold, the RM module instructs the service module to perform a link switchover.

  • If a route in the route monitoring group becomes active again, its down-weight is subtracted from the down-weight sum of the group. When N routes become active again, the sum of their down-weights is subtracted from the down-weight sum of the route monitoring group. When the down-weight sum of the route monitoring group falls below the switchover threshold, the RM module instructs the service module to perform a link switchback. You can set a link switchback delay as required.
As shown in Figure 1, there are 10 routes in total, each with a down-weight of 10. The route monitoring group is associated with service modules A, B, C, and D, and their switchover thresholds are 80, 50, 30, and 20, respectively.
  • When the number of inactive routes in the route monitoring group reaches 2, 3, 5, or 8, the RM module instructs service module D, C, B, or A, respectively, to perform a link switchover.
  • When the number of inactive routes in the route monitoring group falls below 8, 5, 3, or 2, the RM module instructs service module A, B, C, or D, respectively, to perform a link switchback.
