Configuring PoE Power Management

Context

When deploying the PoE function on a network, you need to properly plan the number of connected PDs based on the PoE power supply capability of the device. The calculation method is as follows:

  1. The reference power of a PD is obtained before the PD is newly connected.
  2. Run the display poe-power command to check the available PoE power (the Available power value(mW) field in the command output) and reserved power percentage (the Reserved power percent field in the command output).
  3. You are advised to plan the number of PDs based on the following conditions:

    Sum of the reference power of all PDs on the device ≤ Available power value(mW) x (1 – Reserved power percent)

You can adjust the following power parameters to improve PoE power efficiency and ensure stable system operation.
  • Percentage of reserved PoE power to the available PoE power.

    PD power consumption continuously changes during operation, and can sometimes increase sharply. In such cases, the remaining system power is not sufficient to support the surge in power. Before the system software determines that the PDs connected to low-priority interfaces should be powered off, the PoE power supply will enter the overcurrent protection state due to the overload and cut off the power supply to all connected PDs. You can configure the system reserved power so that it can effectively deal with the surge in power when power consumption increases sharply. Subsequently, the software system has time to power off the PDs connected to low-priority interfaces in order to ensure stable operation of other PDs.

  • Maximum output power of a device

    When the number of PDs connected to a device and power requirements vary, or the total supply power of the device fluctuates, you can configure the maximum output power of the device to ensure that it is controllable.

  • Maximum output power for an interface

    The negotiated power for certain non-standard and unclassifiable PDs may not be the actual power required. You can configure the maximum output power for an interface to prevent a power overload of the connected PD, while also saving power.

Procedure

  1. Enter the system view.

    system-view

  2. Configure the percentage of the reserved PoE power to the total PoE power.

    poe power-reserved power-reserved [ slot slot-id ]

    By default, the percentage of the reserved PoE power to the total PoE power is 20%.

  3. Configure the maximum output power of a device.

    poe max-power max-power slot slot-id

    By default, the maximum output power provided by a PoE device is dynamically adjusted according to the power supply and the input voltage.

  4. Enter the interface view.

    interface interface-type interface-number

  5. Configure the maximum output power of the interface.

    poe power port-max-power

    For details about the maximum output power of an interface, refer to the poe power command in the PoE Configuration Commands.

  6. (Optional) Enable power auto-negotiation on the interface.

    poe power auto-neg enable

    By default, power auto-negotiation is disabled on an interface.

    When the APs connected to the device support both IEEE 802.3at and IEEE 802.3af, enable power auto-negotiation on the interfaces connected to these APs. In some cases, when the total available power of the device is insufficient, the power supply standard of APs is modified to reduce their power consumption. In this way, more APs can be powered.

    Only AP6050 supports power auto-negotiation.

    This command is mutually exclusive with the lldp dot3-tlv power 802.1ab force and lldp dot3-tlv power 802.3at commands.

    When the total available power of the device is less than 30 W, the power supply standards of all the APs connected to interfaces that have power auto-negotiation enabled will be set to IEEE 802.3af. When the total available power of the device is greater than the larger value between 60 W and the reserved power, the device restores the power supply standard of the AP connected to the interface to IEEE 802.3at based on the power supply priority configured on the interface. Specifically, the power supply standard of the AP connected to the interface with a higher priority is restored to IEEE 802.3at first. If the interfaces have the same power supply priority, the power supply standard of the AP connected to the interface with a smaller interface number is restored to IEEE 802.3at first.

    Disabling power auto-negotiation on an interface will automatically restore the power supply standard of the AP connected to the interface to IEEE 802.3at. This operation will power off the APs connected to certain interfaces if the total power of the device is insufficient.
