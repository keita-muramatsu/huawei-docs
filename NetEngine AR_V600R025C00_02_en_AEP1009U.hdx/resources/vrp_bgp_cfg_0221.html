Understanding BGP Peer Tracking

BGP peer tracking provides quick detection of link or peer faults for BGP to speed up network convergence. If BGP peer tracking is configured on a local device and a fault occurs on the link between the device and its peer, BGP peer tracking can quickly detect that the route to the peer is unreachable and notify BGP of the fault, thereby achieving rapid convergence.

Compared with BFD, BGP peer tracking is easy to configure because it needs to be configured only on the local device rather than on the entire network. BGP peer tracking is a fault detection mechanism based on the routing layer, whereas BFD is a fault detection mechanism based on the link layer. Therefore, BGP peer tracking is slower than BFD in convergence and is not applicable to services that require fast convergence, such as voice services.

Networking

BGP peer tracking can quickly detect link or peer faults by checking whether routes to peers exist in the IP routing table. If no route is found in the IP routing table based on the IP address of a BGP peer (or a route exists but is unreachable, for example, because the outbound interface is a Null0 interface), the BGP session goes down, achieving fast BGP route convergence. If a reachable route can be found in this case, the BGP session does not go down.

On the network shown in Figure 1, IGP connections are established among Device A, Device B, and Device C, and a BGP peer relationship is established between Device A and Device C. BGP peer tracking is configured on Device A. When the link between Device A and Device B fails, IGP fast convergence is performed. No route to the IP address of Device C is found on Device A. BGP peer tracking detects that the route to Device C is unreachable and notifies BGP on Device A. Finally, the BGP connection between Device A and Device C is torn down.

Figure 1 Network diagram of BGP peer tracking
  • If a default route exists on Device A and the link between Device A and Device B fails, BGP peer tracking does not tear down the peer relationship between Device A and Device C because Device A can find the default route based on the peer IP address.
  • If Device A and Device C are IBGP peers, you are advised to configure BGP peer tracking on both devices to ensure that the peer relationship can be torn down in time upon a fault.
  • If the establishment of BGP peer relationships depends on IGP routes, you need to set the interval between the time when BGP peer tracking detects that a peer is unreachable and the time when BGP disconnects the connection and ensure that the interval is longer than the IGP route convergence time. Otherwise, before IGP route flapping caused by intermittent disconnection is rectified, the BGP peer relationship may have been torn down, resulting in unnecessary BGP convergence.
