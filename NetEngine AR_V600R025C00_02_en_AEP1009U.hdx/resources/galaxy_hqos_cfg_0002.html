Overview of HQoS

Definition

Hierarchical Quality of Service (HQoS) uses a hierarchical queue scheduling mechanism or hierarchical committed access rate (CAR) mechanism to ensure the bandwidth of different services for users in the Differentiated Services (DiffServ) model.

Purpose

Currently, the device supports two types of HQoS: queue scheduling HQoS and hierarchical CAR HQoS.

Queue Scheduling HQoS

Conventional QoS schedules traffic on a per-interface basis, in which a single interface can differentiate priorities of services but not users. As such, multiple types of traffic with the same priority but from different users are placed into the same port queue (PQ), where they compete for resources. This means that differentiated services cannot be offered to different users.

With the proliferation of users and service types on networks, it becomes ever-more critical to offer differentiated services to users. Queue scheduling HQoS meets this requirement by implementing hierarchical scheduling based on multiple levels of queues, differentiating both services and users and ultimately guaranteeing refined QoS.

Hierarchical CAR HQoS

Traditional CAR implements single-level CAR based on an interface or a certain type of traffic, and cannot provide more refined traffic policing. Hierarchical CAR HQoS can differentiate users and user services, and provide bandwidth guarantee for user services, users, and interfaces.

Based on hierarchical CAR HQoS, you can also configure the intelligent traffic policing function to preferentially guarantee the bandwidth of high-priority service traffic and implement automatic rate limiting policy optimization.

Currently, hierarchical CAR HQoS applies to SD-WAN EVPN scenarios.
