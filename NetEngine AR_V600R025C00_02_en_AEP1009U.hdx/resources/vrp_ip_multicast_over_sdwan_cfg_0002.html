Overview of IP Multicast over SD-WAN

Definition

IP multicast over software-defined wide area network (SD-WAN) is a solution that uses the extended BGP MVPN address family signaling and SD-WAN tunnels to forward multicast traffic data.

A device enabled with IP multicast over SD-WAN uses EVPN IP prefix routes (Type 5 routes) to advertise the Import Site VN-ID attribute. Sites then use Intra-AS I-PMSI auto-discovery (A-D) routes (Type 1 routes) to advertise site information, use Source Active routes (Type 5 routes) to advertise multicast source information to PEs, and use extended BGP MVPN address family signaling (Type 8 routes) to establish SD-WAN tunnels and create (S, G) entries. After receiving the Type 8 routes, the source-side PE forwards multicast traffic through the SD-WAN tunnels. Unicast SD-WAN tunnels are reused on the underlay network to carry multicast traffic.

Purpose

IP multicast over SD-WAN allows a device to forward multicast traffic over SD-WAN tunnels. Currently, enterprises use multicast VPN (MVPN) to deploy multicast services on an existing SD-WAN EVPN and transparently transmit C-multicast traffic to remote VPN sites through the public network. This solution features high reliability and security, and is widely used in service scenarios such as IPTV, video conferencing, and distance education.

Benefits

IP multicast over SD-WAN implements on-demand multicast data forwarding on SD-WAN EVPN networks. It offers the following benefits:

  • Alleviates device performance pressure by reducing broadcast traffic on the SD-WAN EVPN network.

  • Reduces bandwidth consumption on the SD-WAN EVPN network.

  • Guarantees the quality of IP multicast services on the SD-WAN EVPN network.

IP multicast over SD-WAN can be configured with only the following services: SPR in preferential occupation or load balancing mode, SD-WAN EVPN site connection statistics, QoS, and NetStream.
