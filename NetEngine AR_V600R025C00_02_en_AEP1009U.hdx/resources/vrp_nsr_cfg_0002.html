Overview of NSR

Definition

Non-stop routing (NSR) is a reliability technique that prevents the protocol control plane from detecting faults on the system control plane by deploying the active main control board (AMB) and standby main control board (SMB). NSR does not require neighbor assistance, nor does it involve interconnection issues. When an AMB/SMB switchover occurs on a device, its neighbors remain unaware of the switchover.

Purpose

The growing demand for data, audio, and video services poses higher requirements on IP network reliability. Services are complex on an edge device. In many scenarios, especially those involving single-point access, when an AMB/SMB switchover is performed due to a single point of failure or a maintenance operation (for example, a software version upgrade), the edge device cannot converge routing information if its neighbor nodes do not support graceful restart (GR) or they fail simultaneously. NSR helps resolve the preceding problem and ensures uninterrupted forwarding of critical services.

Benefits

NSR prevents route flapping during an AMB/SMB switchover to ensure that services are not affected. NSR satisfies the requirements for high IP network reliability.
