Overview of WLAN QoS

Definition

WLAN Quality of Service (QoS) provides wireless users with differentiated services to meet different traffic requirements. WLAN QoS has the following functions:

  1. Highly efficient use of wireless channels: Enhanced distributed channel access (EDCA) parameters in the Wi-Fi multimedia (WMM) standard are adjusted, enabling high-priority data to preempt wireless channels.
  2. Network congestion reduction: The rate of wireless traffic is limited to prevent packet loss caused by network congestion.
  3. Differentiated services for different types of services: Packets are matched based on access control list (ACL) rules. The same QoS policy is provided for packets of the same type to achieve differentiated services for different types of packets.

Purpose

Applications have differentiated network requirements. A conventional WLAN supports low transmission rates and is therefore mainly used to transmit common data of applications. With the development of emerging WLAN technologies, WLANs have been applied to media, financial, education, and enterprise networks. In addition to common data traffic, WLANs also transmit delay-sensitive multimedia data, such as voice and video. By configuring QoS on a WLAN, the network administrator can properly plan and assign network resources based on service characteristics. The WLAN then provides differentiated access services for applications, meeting customer requirements and improving network utilization.
