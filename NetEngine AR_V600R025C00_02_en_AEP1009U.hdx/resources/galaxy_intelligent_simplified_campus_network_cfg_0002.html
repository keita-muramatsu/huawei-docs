Overview of the Intelligent Simplified Campus Network Solution

Definition

An intelligent simplified campus network consists of a central device and remote units (RUs). These are connected through network cables, optical fibers, or optical/electrical hybrid cables. RUs can expand the interfaces of the central device, and are centrally managed by the central device, implementing a flat network and a simplified architecture.

Purpose

Traditional campus network architectures see the following challenges:

  • High costs: The development of the Internet and Internet of Things (IoT) has diversified the types and increased the number of terminals. Network expansion is necessary to support these new terminals. Currently, devices are deployed in extra-low voltage (ELV) rooms on each floor, and network cables connected to APs are usually buried in walls. Reconstruction and re-cabling are required to connect more terminals, which is costly.
  • Low bandwidth: The rapid development of services such as remote office and online teaching requires more network bandwidth and twisted pair classification. The transmission distance of twisted pairs cannot exceed 100 m, which cannot meet the requirements of ongoing network evolution.
  • Difficult management: Multi-network convergence is trending, making networks and their management more complex.

To meet these challenges, Huawei has launched the intelligent simplified campus network solution. As shown in Figure 1, it consists of a central device and RUs distributed in offices, agile workplaces, and classrooms. The two-layer architecture consisting of a central device and RUs makes network deployment and operations more efficient.

Benefits

  • More interfaces

    As shown in Figure 2, the RU can be regarded as the interface extension of the central device.

    Figure 2 Increasing the number of interfaces
  • Simplified networking

    As shown in Figure 3, the RU on the intelligent simplified campus network can be used as a module of the central device. The RU is plug-and-play and does not need to be configured separately. It is managed by managing the central device. When iMaster NCE-Campus is used for management, only the central device is visible, simplifying the service orchestration logic.

    Figure 3 Simplified networking
  • Flexible deployment

    Using photoelectric PoE, the central device can provide PoE++ power supply for RUs over a distance of up to 300 m. Then, RUs can supply power to connected devices. Remote power supply means that terminal deployment is no longer limited to the availability of power sockets. In addition, the central equipment room features a backup power system (such as UPS), preventing interruptions due to power failures.

  • Energy saving

    The RU does not have a fan, which saves energy. The power consumption of the device is below 8 W, which is far lower than that of traditional access devices.
