Understanding TCP6

Transmission Control Protocol version 6 (TCP6) provides a mechanism to establish virtual circuits between processes of two endpoints. A TCP6 virtual circuit is similar to the full-duplex circuit that transmits data between systems. TCP6 provides reliable data transmission between processes, and is known as a reliable protocol. TCP6 also provides a mechanism to optimize transmission performance according to the network status. When all data can be received and acknowledged, the transmission rate increases gradually. Delay causes the sending host to reduce the sending rate before it receives Acknowledgement packets.

TCP6 is generally used in interactive applications, such as web applications. Certain errors in data receiving affect device running. TCP6 establishes virtual circuits using a three-way handshake mechanism, and all virtual circuits are deleted through a four-way handshake. TCP6 connections provide multiple checksums and reliability functions, but this increases costs. As a result, TCP6 has lower efficiency than User Datagram Protocol version 6 (UDP6).
