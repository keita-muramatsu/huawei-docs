VLAN Deployment

Basic Concepts

In practice, the management VLAN and service VLAN must be configured for transmitting management packets and service data packets, respectively.

  • Management VLAN: transmits packets that are forwarded through CAPWAP tunnels, including management packets and service data packets (in tunnel forwarding mode).
  • Service VLAN: transmits service data packets.

Packet Forwarding Process

In the following figures, VLAN m and VLAN m' represent management VLANs, while VLAN s and VLAN s' represent service VLANs, with the following assumption:
  • When an AP connects to the WAC over Layer 2, VLAN m is the same as VLAN m', and VLAN s is the same as VLAN s'.
  • When an AP connects to the WAC over Layer 3, VLAN m is different from VLAN m', and VLAN s is different from VLAN s'.
Figure 1 shows the process of forwarding management packets through CAPWAP tunnels.
Figure 1 Forwarding management packets through CAPWAP tunnels
  • In the uplink direction (from the AP to the WAC): The AP encapsulates management packets using CAPWAP. After the CAPWAP packets reach Device, it tags the packets with VLAN m. Finally, the WAC removes VLAN m' from the packets and decapsulates the CAPWAP packets.
  • In the downlink direction (from the WAC to the AP): The WAC encapsulates management packets using CAPWAP and tags them with VLAN m'. After the CAPWAP packets reach Device, it removes the tag VLAN m from the packets. Finally, the AP decapsulates the CAPWAP packets.
Figure 2 shows the process of forwarding service data packets in direct forwarding mode.
Figure 2 Forwarding service data packets in direct forwarding mode

In direct forwarding mode, service packets are not encapsulated using CAPWAP.

  • In the uplink direction (from the STA to the Internet): When service data packets in 802.11 format are sent from the STA to the AP, the AP converts the packets into 802.3 packets, tags the packets with VLAN s, and forwards the packets to the destination.
  • In the downlink direction (from the Internet to the STA): When service data packets in 802.3 format reach the AP from the Internet (the packets are tagged with VLAN s' by upper-layer devices), the AP converts the packets into 802.11 packets and sends them to the STA.

VLAN Deployment Suggestions

In actual WLAN networking, management VLANs and service VLANs must be properly planned. The following uses Layer 2 networking between APs and WACs as an example to describe VLAN deployment:

  • As shown in Figure 3, to implement direct forwarding, the AP needs to exchange management VLAN packets with the WAC and also exchange service VLAN packets with upper-layer devices.

    Figure 3 VLAN deployment in direct forwarding mode

  • In direct forwarding mode, it is recommended that different VLANs be used as the management VLAN and service VLAN. Otherwise, service interruption may occur. For example, if the service VLAN and management VLAN are the same, and the management VLAN ID is configured as the PVID for the port connecting the switch to an AP, the service VLAN is terminated when downlink packets are sent out from the switch. As a result, services are interrupted.

  • It is not recommended that VLAN 1 be used as the management VLAN or service VLAN.

  • If VLAN 1 is configured as both the management VLAN and service VLAN, and packets are sent from the uplink ports of APs in untagged mode, you need to configure the VLANIF interfaces corresponding to the address pools for APs and STAs as the PVIDs for ports of switches connected to the APs.
