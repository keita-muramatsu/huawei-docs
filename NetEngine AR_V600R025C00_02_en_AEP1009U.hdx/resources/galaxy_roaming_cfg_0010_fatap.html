Smart Roaming

Overview

Some STAs on live networks have low roaming aggressiveness. As a result, they stick to the initially connected APs even if they move far from the APs and have weak signals or low rates. As a result, these STAs fail to roam to neighbor APs with better signals. Such STAs are called sticky STAs.

Sticky STAs may bring the following problems:
  • Poor service experience: The STAs stick to weak-signal APs, causing a sharp decrease in the wireless channel rate.
  • Channel performance degradation: The STAs have poor signals or low rates, and suffer from frequent packet loss and retransmissions. As a result, the sticky STAs occupy the wireless channels for a long time, and other STAs with better signals cannot obtain sufficient time for using channel resources.

This is where the smart roaming function comes in. After smart roaming is configured, the system proactively steers the STAs to neighboring APs with better signals.

Smart roaming brings the following benefits:

  • Improved service experience and performance: Smart roaming can steer poor-signal STAs to APs with better signals, improving users' service experience and overall channel performance.

  • Load balancing: Smart roaming ensures that each STA is associated with the nearest AP, achieving inter-AP load balancing.

Implementation of Smart Roaming

Smart roaming leverages 802.11k and 802.11v to help STAs determine the roaming time and the target AP during STA roaming.

  • 802.11k, also called neighbor report protocol, enables an AP to scan information about surrounding neighboring APs and works with STAs to scan potential roaming target APs. 802.11k notifies STAs of the list of available APs in advance, so that STAs can select an appropriate target AP when roaming.
  • 802.11v is responsible for roaming transition. In addition to responding to a STA's request for reporting information about neighboring APs, an AP evaluates the connection quality of the STA. It then steers the STA to an AP that provides a better Internet access experience, thereby balancing loads among APs.

  1. Neighbor discovery and maintenance for a STA neighbor list

    An AP collects information about surrounding STAs, discovers neighboring APs, and periodically reports the information to the WAC. The WAC maintains a STA neighbor list based on the information reported by APs. The list contains the neighboring APs of each STA and the corresponding received signal strength indicator (RSSI) and received signal-to-noise indicator (RSNI).

    APs can discover neighboring APs in the following ways:

    • Listening to Probe frames of STAs
    • Periodically switching the operating channel and proactively scanning STAs
    • Requesting STAs to report neighboring APs using the Beacon Report mechanism defined in 802.11k

  2. Sticky STA determination

    When STA_1 associates with AP_1, AP_1 collects the SNR and access rate of STA_1 in real time and determines whether STA_1 is a sticky STA. If STA_1 is identified as a sticky STA, AP_1 reports the STA to the WAC.

  3. Roaming target selection

    After receiving the reported information, the WAC selects the optimal neighboring AP of STA_1 (AP_2) as the target AP to which STA_1 is to roam and delivers the target AP information to AP_1.

  4. Roaming triggering

    AP_1 instructs STA_1 to roam to AP_2 based on the BSS transition mechanism defined in the 802.11v protocol or by disconnecting the STA.

    When STA_1 disassociates from AP_1, AP_1 suppresses the association request from STA_1 temporarily to prevent STA_1 from connecting to AP_1 again.

  5. Roaming completed

    STA_1 roams to AP_2 as expected.

Note that some STAs do not roam to APs with better signals but stick to the initially associated APs even if they are forcibly disconnected. These STAs may even fail to initiate association requests again after being disconnected. The WAC will record these STAs as being unable to roam. When such an unable-to-roam STA is classified as a sticky STA, the AP does not trigger roaming for the STA for a specified duration to prevent service interruption.

In addition to steering sticky STAs, smart roaming provides an optimized roaming steering mechanism for non-sticky STAs to improve the roaming success rate and shorten the roaming period. Specifically, an AP performs measurement to detect better roaming targets for STAs. If such a target is detected for a STA, the AP reports the target to the WAC. The WAC determines the target and delivers a steering message to the AP, which then steers the STA to the target.
