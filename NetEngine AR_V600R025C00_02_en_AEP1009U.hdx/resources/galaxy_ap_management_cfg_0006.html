RU Onboarding Process

An agile distributed WLAN involves the following roles: WAC, central AP, and RU. A WAC centrally manages central APs and RUs, and RUs and central APs are reachable at Layer 2. RUs are remote radio modules of a central AP, and are used for receiving and transmitting 802.11 packets over the air interface. The central AP centrally manages and coordinates RUs on behalf of the WAC, delivering functions such as STA access, configuration delivery, and STA roaming between RUs. The WAC can manage and control central APs and RUs only after they go online.

The RU onboarding process is similar to the AP onboarding process. RUs obtain the WAC's IP address from the central AP and directly establish CAPWAP control tunnels with the WAC.

Discovery phase: An RU searches for an available central AP by sending broadcast packets. The central APs in the broadcast domain return the IP address of the WAC. The RU then unicasts a Discovery Request packet to the WAC. After receiving this unicast packet, the WAC calculates the optimal central AP for the RU, verifies that the RU meets the access control conditions, and sends central AP information to the RU. After receiving central AP information, the RU knows to which the central AP it connects and sends an access request to the WAC.

If multiple central APs in the broadcast domain are online on different WACs, the RU obtains IP addresses of multiple WACs. In this case, the RU obtains information from the WACs, calculates the optimal WAC, and sends an access request to the WAC.

CAPWAP tunnel establishment: A data tunnel and a control tunnel are established between the RU and WAC. In tunnel forwarding mode, a CAPWAP data tunnel is established between the RU and WAC to transmit service data packets. In direct forwarding mode, no CAPWAP data tunnel is established, and service data packets are directly forwarded at Layer 2.
