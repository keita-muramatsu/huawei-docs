Overview of EULA

Definition

An end user license agreement (EULA) is a set of regulations that a software developer or publisher offers to authorize users to use a specific software product. By signing the EULA, users are granted a license to use and benefit from the software. EULAs can take various forms such as a startup pop-up window, command input, screen scrolling display, or paper file.

Purpose

With the expansion of software sales targets and partners, it is no longer realistic for traditional vendors to negotiate and sign contracts with end users in person. As a result, EULAs are commonly used. They can be signed through the input of commands, making it easy to agree on responsibilities in situations where face-to-face communication and negotiation are not possible.
