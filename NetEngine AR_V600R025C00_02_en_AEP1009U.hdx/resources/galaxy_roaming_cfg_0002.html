Overview of Roaming

Definition

Wireless local area network (WLAN) roaming allows wireless stations (STAs) to move within the coverage areas of access points (APs) on the same WLAN with nonstop service transmission. On the network shown in Figure 1, AP_1 and AP_2 use the same network service set identifier (SSID), which is the network name displayed on a STA when it searches for available wireless networks. The coverage areas of AP_1 and AP_2 form an extended service set (ESS). When a STA moves within an ESS, roaming ensures nonstop service transmission.

Figure 1 Roaming networking diagram

Purpose

The most significant advantage of WLAN roaming is that STAs can move within a WLAN without physical media restrictions and without service interruption. Multiple APs are located within an ESS. When a STA moves from the coverage area of an AP to that of another AP in the same ESS, WLAN roaming ensures seamless transition of STA services between APs. WLAN roaming offers the following advantages:

  • Retains STA IP addresses and their services. After roaming, the STAs can still access their initially associated networks.

  • Avoids packet loss or service interruption caused by authentication when STAs associate with a new AP.

Common Roaming Scenarios

A small enterprise provides wireless access services for users through a WLAN and requires service transmission to be uninterrupted when the users move within the enterprise. For this, the enterprise can deploy one WLAN access controller (WAC) and multiple APs, and configure the WAC to manage the APs and provide WLAN services for users. In Figure 2, the enterprise deploys a WAC to manage APs. The STA can connect to the WLAN through AP_1 and AP_2. When the STA roams from AP_1 to AP_2, services are not interrupted.
