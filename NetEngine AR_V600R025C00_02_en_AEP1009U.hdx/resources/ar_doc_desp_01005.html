WAN Interconnection

ARs make WAN access easy by providing abundant interfaces — GE, E1, Packet over SDH (POS), VDSL, and 3G/LTE/5G cellular interfaces — which fit different kinds of networks provided by carriers. In addition, ARs offer dual uplinks to implement interface backup, delivering highly reliable WAN interconnection.

Available WAN interfaces on ARs depend on the device model and the boards installed.

On the network shown in Figure 1, the Router functions as the gateway at the HQ of an SME, and RouterA, RouterB, and RouterC function as gateways at branches of the SME. Enterprise branches connect to the HQ in wired or wireless mode.

  • ARs support wireless WAN access through 3G/LTE/5G interfaces as well as cellular channel interfaces, in compliance with 3GPP specifications. This allows for wireless interconnection between enterprise branches and between enterprise branches and the HQ.
  • ARs can deliver WAN access through wired GE and 10GE links, with wireless 3G/LTE/5G links as backup. This enables interconnection between enterprise branches as well as between enterprise branches and the HQ, significantly boosting network stability while reducing network construction costs.
Figure 1 Connecting enterprise branches to the Internet through multiple types of links

On the network shown in Figure 2, the Router functions as the HQ gateway of an electric power company, and RouterA and RouterB are branch gateways. They are connected through the SDH network. The branches use POS interfaces to transmit data at a high speed to the HQ. High-level Data Link Control (HDLC) can be configured on the routers to implement communication between gateways in the HQ and branches.

Figure 2 Connecting branches and the HQ through POS private lines

On the network shown in Figure 3, RouterA acts as the HQ gateway and RouterB as the branch gateway. The branch connects to the HQ through E1 private lines. Point-to-Point Protocol (PPP) can implement communication between them.
