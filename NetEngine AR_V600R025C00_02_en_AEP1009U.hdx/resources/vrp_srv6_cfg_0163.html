Understanding TE-Class-based Traffic Steering into an SRv6 TE Policy

TE-Class IDs can be used to identify flows, helping differentiate the flows and steer them into different SPR policies, SRv6 TE Policies, or native IP links.

Smart policy routing (SPR) is a route selection mechanism that proactively detects link quality and selects the optimal link to forward service data according to service quality requirements, implementing intelligent traffic steering. For more information about SPR, see "Smart Policy Routing Configuration" in Configuration Guide > QoS Configuration.

TE-Class-based Traffic Steering

In TE-Class-based traffic steering, the headend recurses traffic based on the next hop of the corresponding route in compliance with the configured tunnel policy. If traffic recursion to an SRv6 TE flow group is configured, the headend matches the traffic with an SRv6 mapping policy that has the same color value as the route. If such an SRv6 mapping policy exists, the headend dynamically generates an SRv6 TE flow group that contains multiple SRv6 TE Policies with the same endpoint but different color values.

If there is no such SRv6 mapping policy, the headend does not dynamically create an SRv6 TE flow group.

If multiple flows with the same color value but different next-hop addresses need to recurse to SRv6 TE flow groups, multiple dynamic SRv6 TE flow groups with different endpoints are created based on the configuration of the matching SRv6 mapping policy.

Figure 1 shows the TE-Class-based traffic steering process.

Figure 1 TE-Class-based traffic steering
The traffic steering process is as follows:
  1. SRv6 TE Policy 1 and SRv6 TE Policy 2 are delivered to headend device A through a controller. The color values for SRv6 TE Policy 1 and SRv6 TE Policy 2 are 123 and 124, respectively. The endpoints of the policies are device B's address 2001:DB8:1::1.
  2. Device B sends the BGP route 2001:DB8:90::/80 to device A through a BGP peer relationship.
  3. A tunnel selection policy and SRv6 mapping policy are configured on device A. The mapping type of the SRv6 mapping policy is set to TE-Class, and a rule is defined for the mapping between SPR policies, SRv6 TE Policies, or native IP links in an SRv6 TE flow group and TE-Class values. Based on these configurations, the device dynamically generates an SRv6 TE flow group for services.
  4. On device A, services are associated with the SRv6 TE flow group based on the next hop of the involved service route. These services carry TE-Class information during forwarding. Based on the SRv6 mapping policy configuration, the services are first associated with a specific color and then a specific SPR policy, SRv6 TE Policy, or native IP link in the flow group, thereby implementing TE-Class-based traffic steering.
