Traffic policy

The sequence for traffic policies to take effect is described as follows:

For the AR5710-H8T2TS1-T, AR5710-H4T4N2X, AR6710-L8T3TS1X2, AR6710-L8T3TS1X2-T, AR5710-H2T4PN2X-NRGL, AR5710-H4T4N2X-LTE4EA, AR5710-H8T2TS1, AR5710-H2T4N2X-NRGL:

Software-based forwarding mode:

- Traffic policies applied in different views can take effect simultaneously. If actions conflict (for example, deny and redirection), traffic policies in the following views take effect in descending order of priority: sub-interface/VLANIF interface > interface.

Hardware-based forwarding mode:

- Traffic policies applied in different views can take effect simultaneously. If actions conflict (for example, deny and redirection), traffic policies in the following views take effect in descending order of priority: sub-interface > interface > VLANIF.

For the AR8000 series, AR5710-S10T1X2, AR5710-S28T2S2XE4, AR5710-S52T2XE4, AR5710-S8P1XDSE-2LTE4EA, AR5710-S8P2X, AR5710-S8P2XE-NRGL, AR5710-S8T1XWE, AR5710-S8T1XWE-NRGL, AR5710-S8T2S, AR5710-S8T2X, AR5710-S8T2X-LTE4EA, AR5710-S8T2XE, AR5710-S8T2XE-LTE4EA-T, AR5710-S8T2XE-NRGL, AR5710-S8TWDSE-LTE4EA, AR6710-H4T4X2Y7, AR6710-L14T2X4, AR6710-L26T2X4, AR6710-L26T2X4-T, AR6710-L50T2X4, AR6710-L50T2X4-T:

- The priority of a traffic policy on a sub-interface or VLANIF interface is higher than that of a traffic policy on an interface.

Traffic policy

Traffic policy resource usage:

- To apply a traffic policy, the device must have sufficient ACL resources. Otherwise, the traffic policy fails to be applied. The ACL resource usage depends on the configuration.

(1) If if-match acl and if-match ipv6 acl are configured in a traffic classifier, a single rule in an ACL group occupies one ACL resource. If a Layer 4 port number range is configured in a rule, the rule is split. You can run the display system tcam acl port-division command to query the number of split rules. If tcp-flag established is configured in a rule, the rule occupies two ACL resources. If the rule references an IP address pool or Layer 4 port number pool, the number of ACL resources occupied by the rule depends on the pool configuration.

(2) If if-match vlan/inner-vlan is configured in a traffic classifier, the number of occupied ACL resources is affected by the VLAN ID range. You can run the display system tcam acl port-division command to query the number of split ACL rules.

(3) Multiple values can be specified for an if-match matching rule in a traffic classifier. For example, if if-match 8021p 2 3 is configured, the number of occupied ACL resources is the same as the number of values.

(4) The AND and OR modes in a traffic policy affect ACL resource usage. In AND mode, all if-match matching rules except if-match acl/ipv6 acl are combined with ACL rules. For example, if if-match acl 3000 and if-match source-mac are configured in a traffic classifier and three rules are configured in ACL 3000 (assuming that there are three rules after splitting), three ACL resources are occupied in AND mode, and four ACL resources are occupied in OR mode. Note that different values of the same if-match matching rule cannot be combined. For example, if if-match acl 3000 and if-match vlan 1 to 2 are configured in a traffic classifier, three rules are configured in ACL 3000 (assuming that there are three rules after splitting), and two rules are occupied after vlan 1 to 2 is split. In AND mode, six (3 x 2 = 6) ACL resources are occupied. (A flow cannot match both VLAN 1 and VLAN 2. Therefore, three rules are generated by combining VLAN 1 and ACL rules, and another three rules are generated by combining VLAN 2 and ACL rules.) In OR mode, five ACL resources are occupied.

Traffic policy

When a traffic policy is applied to an interface:

- When a traffic policy is applied to an Eth-Trunk interface, the traffic policy is re-delivered if a member interface is updated. In this case, the traffic policy that has been applied becomes invalid temporarily.

- When a traffic policy is applied to a Layer 3 main interface, only Layer 3 unicast traffic is supported.

Traffic policy

When a traffic policy is applied or the data of an applied traffic policy is modified or deleted, there is a delay between the time when the configuration is committed and the time when the configuration takes effect. The more the rules, the longer the delay. In extreme cases, the delay for the configuration to take effect may reach the minute level.

Traffic policy

MQC does not support Layer 2 traffic.

Traffic policy

If a traffic policy takes effect for protocol packets, the protocol packets may be discarded.

Traffic policy

When a traffic policy is applied to a VLANIF interface:

- Services such as VRRP are not supported.

- The traffic classifier bound to the traffic policy cannot define a matching rule based on the destination MAC address.

- The traffic policy takes effect only for Layer 3 unicast traffic.

Traffic policy

Traffic policies configured on the device do not take effect for packets that are discarded due to local attack defense.

Traffic policy

Limitations on using MQC in MPLS scenarios:

A traffic policy takes effect on the ingress, transit, and egress nodes of a tunnel.

Traffic policy

Limitations on applying a traffic policy to a Layer 3 sub-interface:

- Matching rules based on interface and VLAN information cannot be configured.

- If the traffic policy fails to be applied to the sub-interface because the sub-interface configuration is incomplete, no message is displayed. You can run the display traffic-policy applied-record command to check the application status.

- Only Layer 3 unicast traffic is supported.

Traffic policy

A traffic policy does not take effect for mirrored traffic.

Traffic policy

When preprocessing or preferential processing traffic policies are applied:

- The traffic behaviors in a preprocessing traffic policy support only the statistics enable (traffic behavior view) and deny | permit commands.

- The traffic behaviors in a preferential processing traffic policy support only the statistics enable (traffic behavior view), deny | permit, car (traffic behavior view), and remark dscp commands.

- The following fields can be defined in preprocessing and preferential processing traffic policies: IPv4 address, IPv6 address, port number, protocol type, ToS, DSCP, fragment flag, TCP flag, VPN instance, application, and application group.

Traffic policy

Services preempt ACL and statistics resources. After the device is reset, resources may be insufficient for configuration restoration. As a result, traffic policies fail to be delivered.

Traffic policy

When traffic policing and another flow action are defined in different traffic behaviors of the same traffic policy and priorities of matching traffic classifiers are different, only the action corresponding to the high-priority traffic classifier takes effect if packets match multiple traffic classifiers. In this case, rate limiting may fail.

Traffic policy

For the AR5710-H4T4N2X, AR5710-H8T2TS1-T, AR6710-L8T3TS1X2, AR6710-L8T3TS1X2-T, AR5710-H2T4PN2X-NRGL, AR5710-H4T4N2X-LTE4EA, AR5710-H8T2TS1, AR5710-H2T4N2X-NRGL:

If the forwarding plane is switched, all traffic policies that have been applied are re-delivered.

Traffic policy

For the AR5710-H4T4N2X, AR5710-H8T2TS1-T, AR6710-L8T3TS1X2, AR6710-L8T3TS1X2-T, AR5710-H2T4PN2X-NRGL, AR5710-H4T4N2X-LTE4EA, AR5710-H8T2TS1, AR5710-H2T4N2X-NRGL:

The main differences between software-based and hardware-based MQC are as follows:

(1) If both the parent and child policies are configured with mirroring, the child policy takes effect in hardware-based forwarding scenarios; both the parent and child policies take effect and two copies of packets are replicated in software-based forwarding scenarios.

(2) If flow mirroring, redirection, and packet filtering are configured, in hardware-based forwarding scenarios, mirroring takes effect only in the inbound direction; in software-based forwarding scenarios, mirroring takes effect in both inbound and outbound directions.

(3) If flow mirroring and traffic policing are both configured, all packets are mirrored in software-based forwarding scenarios, and the packets after traffic policing are mirrored in hardware-based forwarding scenarios.

(4) If MQC is configured on both the inbound physical and logical interfaces, the software-based forwarding process executes MQC on the physical interface first and then on the logical interface, whereas the hardware-based forwarding process executes MQC on the logical interface first and then on the physical interface. The execution results are different.

(5) Hardware-based forwarding supports redirection only in the inbound direction.

(6) In hardware-based forwarding scenarios, only Layer 4 information can be matched for the first fragment.

Traffic behavior

Packet filtering action:

- If deny is configured in the ACL rule referenced by the traffic classifier or the deny action is configured in the traffic behavior, the matched traffic is denied.

Traffic behavior

In traffic statistics, the number of bytes includes the inter-frame gap and preamble.

Traffic behavior

Limitations on MQC-based traffic statistics collection:

- Statistics are updated at an interval of 5 seconds.

Traffic classifier

Limitation on traffic classification based on VLANs:

- It cannot be applied to sub-interfaces.

Traffic classifier

A traffic classifier in which matching rules are ANDed cannot define two or more of the following rules: IP precedence, DSCP value, and CoS value.

Traffic classifier

For a traffic classifier defining IPv4 ACL rules:

- If traffic classifiers bound to a traffic policy define matching rules based on the TCP port number, UDP port number, or fragment flag and the traffic policy is applied to the outbound direction, fragmented packets may be incorrectly matched.

- Matching based on the TTL field is not supported.

- IPv4 ACL rules and IPv6 ACL rules cannot be configured in the same traffic policy.

Traffic classifier

Traffic classification based on the QoS local ID:

- In a traffic policy, if-match qos-local-id cannot be configured together with if-match ipv6 acl or if-match ipv6 dscp.
