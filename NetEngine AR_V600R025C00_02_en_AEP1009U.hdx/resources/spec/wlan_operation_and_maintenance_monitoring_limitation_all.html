CSI sensing

For the AR8000 series, AR6700 series, AR5710-S52T2XE4, AR5710-H8T2TS1-T, AR5710-S8T2X-LTE4EA, AR5710-S8T2X, AR5710-H2T4PN2X-NRGL, AR5710-S8P2X, AR5710-S8T2S, AR5710-H4T4N2X, AR5710-S10T1X2, AR5710-S28T2S2XE4, AR5710-H4T4N2X-LTE4EA, AR5710-S8P2XE-NRGL, AR5710-H8T2TS1, AR5710-S8P1XDSE-2LTE4EA, AR5710-S8T2XE-NRGL, AR5710-S8T2XE-LTE4EA-T, AR5710-H2T4N2X-NRGL, AR5710-S8T2XE:

1. In indoor open energy-saving scenarios, 4T4R APs are recommended. The inter-site distance of APs must be less than 12 m to ensure that the detection covers the whole area. If this distance exceeds 12 m, coverage holes may exist in the detection range.

2. In indoor open energy-saving scenarios, the application system must consider the detection results of multiple APs in the area and make a comprehensive energy-saving judgment based on the specific environment and area division. Precise light control is not supported. Suggestions:

2.1. Strongly recommended: The CSI sensing function provides statistics about human presence and absence. The application system determines a fixed energy-saving policy based on the statistics, for example, turning off air conditioners from 10:00 p.m. to 6:00 a.m. the next day.

2.2. Appropriate use: The CSI sensing function provides real-time data for turning off lights in an area. The application system determines continuous human absence within a long period of no less than 30 minutes.

2.3. Appropriate use: If the application system needs to associate all the lights in an area, all APs in the area need to be associated. When human presence is detected by any AP, all lights are turned on. The lights in the area need be manually controlled by buttons, which cannot strongly depend on CSI sensing. Otherwise, the lights cannot be turned on in a timely manner.

CSI sensing

For the AR8000 series, AR6700 series, AR5710-S52T2XE4, AR5710-H8T2TS1-T, AR5710-S8T2X-LTE4EA, AR5710-S8T2X, AR5710-H2T4PN2X-NRGL, AR5710-S8P2X, AR5710-S8T2S, AR5710-H4T4N2X, AR5710-S10T1X2, AR5710-S28T2S2XE4, AR5710-S8TWDSE-LTE4EA, AR5710-H4T4N2X-LTE4EA, AR5710-S8P2XE-NRGL, AR5710-H8T2TS1, AR5710-S8P1XDSE-2LTE4EA, AR5710-S8T2XE-NRGL, AR5710-S8T2XE-LTE4EA-T, AR5710-H2T4N2X-NRGL, AR5710-S8T2XE:

1. The CSI sensing function can operate only on radios with Wi-Fi services enabled. When an AP is configured to enter the power-saving mode or its radios are disabled, the CSI sensing function of the AP becomes unavailable.

2. If there are continuously moving objects or living beings within the sensing area, such as fans, robotic vacuum cleaners, curtains with metallic fittings (high reflection coefficient), drapes, mechanically vibrating equipment, trees outside the window, or pets, the system may misinterpret these as human motions, micro-motions, or breathing. This can lead to false detection or cause the CSI sensing function to fail. Therefore, ensure that the environment is free of such interference sources.

3. The CSI sensing distance is affected by obstacles in the detection area. For details, see the path loss evaluation table for CSI sensing in the network planning guide. If obstacles that cause large penetration loss of Wi-Fi signals (such as concrete walls and solid wood doors/tables) completely block signals, the CSI sensing function is unavailable.

4. CSI sensing is supported only on a 5 GHz or 6 GHz radio but not on a 2.4 GHz radio, and requires the radio bandwidth of at least 40 MHz. If such requirements are not met, the CSI sensing function is unavailable. The 20 MHz frequency bandwidth affects the ranging accuracy and cannot ensure the accuracy of the specified detection range.

5. The detection range of CSI sensing is circular, which is a hemisphere in the direction of the AP antenna. When the AP is deployed in a room with walls that Wi-Fi signals can easily penetrate (for example, glass, thin wooden panels, or gypsum board), the following limitations apply:

5.1 If the door is located in a corner, you need to walk one or two steps inside the room after opening the door to trigger the light on. To prevent lights from being triggered by mistake when people walk outside the walls, it is recommended that the light-on distance be set to the radius of the inscribed circle.

5.2 When people pass close to a glass wall outside the room, there is a low probability (less than 1%) that the light is turned on. (The distance measurement error is about 1 m, which cannot be completely eliminated.)

5.3 False detection may occur in edge areas (such as corridors) outside the room or adjacent rooms. Continuous human movement outside may cause delayed light-off or failure to turn off the light. In the worst case, the light can be turned off at night when no one is present. To ensure that the lights are not turned off by mistake in all areas of the room, it is recommended that the light-off distance be set to the radius of the circumscribed circle. If there is continuous interference outside the room, set the light-off distance to the radius of the inscribed circle. In this case, a small range of missed detection may exist in the four corners of the room.

5.4 If a person remains standing or sitting still in the corridor outside for about 1 minute, the system may falsely trigger the light inside the room. This is due to the inability of breathing detection to measure the distance accurately when the detection target is in stationary state. Larger body or limb movements will not cause false detection.

6. If existing AP locations are not adjusted for CSI network planning, or if the room layout is irregular or deployment is non-standard, CSI sensing may result in over-coverage or blind spots, and the CSI sensing effect cannot be guaranteed. In such cases, it is recommended that CSI sensing be configured only for light-off control, with detection distance covering the entire room, while tolerating occasional false detections outside. In the worst case, lights can still be turned off at night when no one is present.

7. If the person sensing distance range on exit (presence-leave-range) is greater than the person sensing distance range on entry (presence-enter-range), the human presence aging time configured on the AP must be set to at least more than 10 minutes. If the aging time on the AP is too short, for example, 1 minute, when a person stays in the inner and outer circles of the detection range for more than 1 minute, the human absence state is determined, and human presence cannot be identified unless the person enters the inner circle again.

CSI sensing

In outdoor scenarios, there are many types of interfering objects and the scenarios are complex, increasing the number of false CSI sensing events increases. This function is not supported currently.

CSI sensing

1. When an AP is deployed in a conference room with partition walls made of glass, thin wood boards, or gypsum boards, Wi-Fi signals can easily penetrate the walls. In this case, signals are extended to the outside of the conference room and are easily affected by passers-by, causing misjudgments. Therefore, the AP must be deployed at least 3 m away from glass walls, controlling the sensing range within the room, and the distance sensing range can be adjusted to reduce misjudgments. For details, see the network planning guide.

2. In a conference room, there may be breath-like interference such as wind-blown curtains and disturbances outside glass walls. Therefore, you are advised to disable breath detection to prevent continuous misjudgments as human breaths.

3. In energy-saving scenarios such as conference rooms, the human absence-to-presence sensing delay is 3s (device: 2.3s, including 0.2s as the reserved network transmission delay, and 0.5s for other upper-layer applications, ensuring that the total delay meets the requirements of network transmission or application processing).

4. In conference room scenarios, motion and micro-motion detection is supported, allowing recognition of small actions such as raising the head, lifting a hand, or shifting the body. However, detection is not supported when the human body remains completely still or when only hand movements occur. Therefore, it is recommended that the application system should determine no person in a conference room when there is no person for more than 10 minutes, and then perform energy-saving processing such as turning off lights. Alternatively, set the presence aging time for APs to more than 10 minutes. For details about network planning and deployment in conference scenarios, see the network planning guide.

5. If the distance sensing sensitivity level needs to be adjusted due to partition walls (such as glass partitions), the sensing radio channel must be fixed and the AP installation direction must remain unchanged before the sensitivity adjustment. If the channel or direction is changed, the sensitivity needs to be re-adjusted. Note: In R025C00 and later versions, this limitation is eliminated because it is recommended that the sending distance range be configured.

CSI sensing

1. To ensure effective coverage of a detection area, it is recommended that the AP be installed in the middle of the ceiling. If the AP cannot be ceiling mounted, ensure that the AP faces the detection area. Additionally, ensure that the surrounding area of the AP is open without obstacles, and there is no highly reflective object such as large metal material within 1 m in the detection direction, preventing the coverage radius from being affected. Do not install the AP in ceilings or decorative panels made of solid wood, metal, or other high-penetration-loss materials. Otherwise, the detection radius will be too small, causing the function to fail.

2. Ensure that the AP is installed in a secure, stable position free from vibration. For ceiling mounting scenarios, ensure that the ceiling itself is vibration-free. Any shaking of the AP may result in false detection.

3. Ensure that the AP is installed more than 30 cm away from devices that generate electromagnetic interference or are equipped with fans (such as legacy fluorescent lamps, projectors, and air-conditioner outlets) to prevent false detection.

4. The CSI sensing range is circular, which is a hemisphere in the direction of the AP antenna. The sensing radius R is configurable, which places strict requirements on network planning and AP placement. In rooms with multiple glass surfaces, thin wooden partitions, or gypsum board walls, installation accuracy is critical. If precise placement is not possible, the maximum position deviation must be less than 0.5 m, and the configuration error for radius R must be less than 0.5 m.

Upon insufficient power supply or manual configuration, the number of spatial streams decreases and radios are shut down for some AP models. In this case, the CSI sensing distance may decrease from the 4T4R specification to the 2T2R specification or the CSI sensing becomes unavailable. Therefore, when using the CSI sensing function, ensure that the power supply is sufficient and 4T4R radio services are enabled. For details about the impact, you can access Info-Finder, search for the AP model, and search for power supply description in hardware specifications to check whether the radio supports the 4T4R capability at the corresponding power supply level.

CSI sensing

Networking and radio bandwidth limitations of CSI sensing

xx76 series models:

1. CSI sensing does not support 2.4 GHz radios. Only 5 GHz and 6 GHz radios are supported, and the radio bandwidth must be at least 40 MHz. Otherwise, CSI sensing is unavailable. The 20 MHz small frequency bandwidth affects the ranging precision and cannot ensure the accuracy of the limited detection range.

2. For models with 4T4R radios, the CSI sensing function is not supported on other 2T2R radios. To use the CSI sensing function, ensure that the 4T4R radio is enabled. (If the 4T4R radio supports only 6 GHz, the CSI sensing function is available only in countries or regions that support 6 GHz.) For models with only 2T2R radios, the CSI sensing function is supported only by radio 2 (third radio). To use this function, ensure that radio 2 (third radio) is enabled. For details about the capability, see the specifications.

3. When the 320 MHz bandwidth is configured for a 6 GHz radio, the CSI sensing function is not supported. Configure a bandwidth of 160 MHz or lower. Otherwise, the detection distance specification may decrease (other 2T2R radios are selected) or the CSI sensing function may be unavailable (no other detection radios are available).

xx73 series models:

1. CSI sensing supports only 5 GHz radios, and the radio bandwidth must be at least 80 MHz. Otherwise, the function is unavailable.

2. When CSI awareness is enabled, the channel utilization of the air interface is less than 3%. In addition, the channel utilization of Wi-Fi services must be less than 50%. Otherwise, false detection may occur.

CSI sensing

1. Radar installation must follow the "one room, one plan" positioning requirement, aligned with the hotel's installation baseline. To ensure respiratory detection accuracy, the recommended deployment positions are: a. On the TV wall directly facing the bed, vertically 10 cm above the bed height, and horizontally offset less than 30 cm from the bed edge. b. Ceiling-mounted at the center of the room. For details, see the network planning guide.

2. The AP cannot be installed behind a TV, behind metal decorative panels, or inside thick wooden cabinets. Thin wooden panels or gypsum board can be used for decorative concealment.

3. Ensure that no air-conditioning outlet is located within 1 meter around the AP to prevent false detections caused by airflow.

4. When the AP is wall-mounted in a concealed manner, the area directly adjacent to the wall on the mounting side lies in the antenna's side-lobe null zone, where micro-motion detection may be ineffective.

5. A centrally deployed AP cannot cover the doorway area, which may affect light-switch automation. As such, an additional independent sensor needs to be installed at the doorway for linked detection.

6. Solid walls or partitions of bathrooms cause significant penetration loss of signals. Additional independent sensors are required to cover these areas for linked detection.

7. Suites or large resort hotel rooms cannot be fully covered by a single AP. In this case, multiple APs are required for a room.

8. In hotel scenarios, the system supports motion and micro-motion detection, capable of recognizing small actions such as head raising, hand waving, or body swaying. Static (respiratory) detection is only supported directly in front of the AP (bed area). To avoid missed detections when transitioning from the human presence to absence state, it is recommended that the energy-saving application system require continuous detection of human absence for more than 10 minutes before triggering actions such as turning off lights, or configure the human absence aging time on the AP to at least 10 minutes.

9. If there is a solid wall or partition with an attenuation greater than 5 dB between the AP and the doorway, and the presence aging time is less than 10 minutes, it is recommended that an independent sensor be added at the doorway to prevent missed detections.

CSI sensing

Model limitations of CSI sensing in different scenarios

1. In conference rooms and indoor open areas, this function is supported only by AirEngine XX76 series models due to the limitation of the minimum bandwidth supported by the CSI sensing function.

2. In hotel scenarios, this function is supported only by AirEngine XX73 series models due to the limitation of the breath detection capability supported by the CSI sensing function.
