802.11r Fast Roaming

Overview

On a WLAN, when a STA moves between different APs, the service continuity and data transmission stability must be ensured. In traditional roaming technologies, when a STA roams from one AP to another, the STA needs to re-perform identity authentication and key negotiation. As a result, services are interrupted for a long time during roaming, degrading user experience. To solve this problem, the IEEE 802.11r standard is introduced. 802.11r, or Fast Basic Service Set Transition (FT), allows a STA to complete authentication and key negotiation with the target AP in advance. This process skips 802.1X authentication and key negotiation during roaming, reducing the number of information exchanges and achieving low latency and service data flow continuity during roaming.

Implementation of 802.11r Fast Roaming

802.11r fast roaming can be performed in Over-the-Air or Over-the-DS mode. The two modes are similar, differing only in the FT authentication process. In Over-the-Air mode, the STA directly performs FT authentication with the FAP. In Over-the-DS mode, the STA performs FT authentication with the FAP through the HAP.

The following uses the Over-the-Air mode as an example to describe the intra-WAC 802.11r fast roaming process, as shown in Figure 1.

Figure 1 Flowchart of intra-WAC 802.11r fast roaming (Over-the-Air)
  1. First access: A STA accesses the network through AP_1 for the first time and is successfully authenticated by the WAC. During this process, a PMK is generated.

    Key generation and distribution: The WAC generates PMK-R0 and PMK-R1 of each AP based on the PMK, and delivers PMK-R1 to AP_1. PMK-R0 is calculated based on the SSID, MDID, WAC MAC address, and STA MAC address. PMK-R1 is calculated based on the PMK-R0, AP MAC address, and STA MAC address.

    Key negotiation: The STA and WAC negotiate keys to generate and install the pairwise transient key (PTK) and group temporal key (GTK).

  2. FT authentication request: The STA initiates an FT authentication request to AP_2 during roaming, and sends PMK-R1 to AP_2.
  3. FT authentication response: After receiving the request, AP_2 generates and installs a PTK according to PMK-R1 and information contained in the request frame. In addition, AP_2 starts the reassociation timer, and sends an 802.11 FT authentication response to the STA.
  4. FT reassociation request: After receiving the response, the STA generates and installs a PTK based on the information contained in the response frame. The STA sends a reassociation request to AP_2.
  5. FT reassociation response: After receiving the reassociation request, AP_2 disables the reassociation timer, and then sends a reassociation response to the STA.

    If a STA blacklist or whitelist is configured on the WAC, the AP sends a reassociation response to the STA during FT reassociation and then reports the STA's reassociation request to the WAC for processing.

  6. Roaming completion: After receiving the response, the STA completes the roaming process, and the service data flow is continuous.
