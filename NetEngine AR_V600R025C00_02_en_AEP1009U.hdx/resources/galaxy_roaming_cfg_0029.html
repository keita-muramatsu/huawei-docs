AI Roaming

Overview

AI roaming is an enhancement of smart roaming technology. A WAC provides personalized steering policies for different types of STAs and starts steering at a proper time through the coordinated measurement mechanism. This improves the roaming sensitivity and success rate as well as resolving sticky STA issues.

Implementation of AI Roaming

The WAC continuously detects the received signal strength indicator (downlink RSSI) of a STA to predict its possible roaming paths. Based on the RSSI differences between the associated AP and neighboring APs, the WAC steers the STA to a neighboring AP with better signal strength.

Figure 1 shows the implementation process of AI roaming.

Figure 1 Flowchart of AI roaming
  1. STA profile matching

    After a STA goes online, the WAC delivers STA profile information to the associated AP.

    As STAs of different vendors and models have various roaming behaviors, fixed steering policies on the AP side cannot provide consistent steering effects for the STAs, thereby making it difficult to ensure roaming experience. With the AI-powered reinforcement learning function, Huawei analyzes the roaming behaviors of mainstream STAs and abstracts an effective steering policy for each type of STAs into different STA profiles (including the protocol capabilities, roaming steering conditions, and roaming steering parameters). Each STA profile corresponds to a personalized steering policy. In this manner, a STA profile library is formed.

  2. RSSI measurement by the associated AP

    When a STA moves, the RSSI from the associated AP typically changes from weak, to strong, and then to weak. To promptly detect the RSSI change of the STA, the associated AP periodically sends an RSSI measurement request to the STA. Upon receiving the response packet from the STA, the associated AP obtains the measured RSSI and reports it to the WAC. By continuously measuring the RSSIs of the STA and comparing them against the features of the corresponding STA profile, the associated AP can determine whether the STA is moving away from it.

  3. Coordinated measurement by neighboring APs

    If the associated AP determines that the STA is moving away, it sends a coordinated measurement request to possible neighboring APs. The neighboring APs then use the independent scanning radio to send RSSI measurement requests to the STA. The STA measures the RSSIs of the neighboring APs and reports the RSSIs to the associated AP. The associated AP then reports the coordinated measurement results to the WAC.

  4. Steering

    After receiving the measurement results, if the WAC detects that the signal strength of a neighboring AP is significantly stronger than that of the associated AP, the WAC initiates 802.11v roaming steering and uses the STA profile matched by the STA to steer the STA to the target neighboring AP.

  • The roaming steering function based on coordinated measurement requires that STAs support LM (802.11k) or TPC measurement (802.11h) and roaming steering (802.11v).
  • The roaming steering function based on coordinated measurement requires Layer 2 network connectivity between APs.
  • APs must support independent radio scanning to perform steering based on coordinated measurement. For APs that do not support independent radio scanning, after the AI roaming function is enabled, the STA profiling mechanism is used to perform personalized steering for STAs based on the triggering logic of smart roaming.
