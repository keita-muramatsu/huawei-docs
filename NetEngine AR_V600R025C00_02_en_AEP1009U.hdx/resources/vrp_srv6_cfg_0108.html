Configuring Color-based Traffic Steering into an SRv6 TE Policy

Context

After an SRv6 TE Policy is configured, traffic needs to be steered into it for forwarding. This process is called traffic steering. Currently, SRv6 TE Policies can be used for various services, such as BGP L3VPN and EVPN services. This section describes how to configure services to recurse to an SRv6 TE Policy through a specified tunnel policy.

Procedure

  1. Configure a tunnel policy.
    1. Enter the system view.

      system-view

    2. Create a tunnel policy and enter the tunnel policy view.

      tunnel-policy policy-name

    3. (Optional) Configure a description for the tunnel policy.

      description description-information

    4. Configure the tunnel selection sequence and the number of tunnels for load balancing.

      tunnel select-seq ipv6 srv6-te-policy load-balance-number loadBalanceNumber

      After the preceding configurations are complete, routes are allowed to recurse to SRv6 TE Policies. If the color and next hop of a route are the same as the color and endpoint of an SRv6 TE Policy, respectively, the route can successfully recurse to the SRv6 TE Policy. In this case, the traffic forwarded through the route can be steered into the SRv6 TE Policy.

  2. Configure service recursion to an SRv6 TE Policy.
    • Configure EVPN L3VPN service recursion to an SRv6 TE Policy.
      1. Enter the VPN instance view.
        ip vpn-instance vpn-instance-name
      2. Enter the VPN instance IPv4/IPv6 address family view.
        IPv4:
ipv4-family
IPv6:
ipv6-family
      3. Apply the tunnel policy to an EVPN L3VPN instance.
        tnl-policy policy-name evpn
    • Configure public network IPv4/IPv6 service recursion to an SRv6 TE Policy.
      1. Create a tunnel selector and enter the tunnel selector view.
        tunnel-selector name matchMode node node
      2. Apply the tunnel policy to the tunnel selector.
        apply tunnel-policy tunnel-policy-name
      3. Return to the system view.
        quit
      4. Enter the BGP view.
        bgp as-number
      5. Enter the IPv4/IPv6 unicast address family view.
        IPv4:
ipv4-family unicast
IPv6:
ipv6-family unicast
      6. Enable the device to recurse IPv4/IPv6 unicast routes received from a BGP IPv6 peer to tunnels.
        unicast-route recursive-lookup tunnel-v6 tunnel-selector tunnel-selector-name
