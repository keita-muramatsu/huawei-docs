Understanding BGP Route Dampening

Route instability is reflected by route flapping, whereby a route repeatedly appears and disappears in the routing table.

When route flapping occurs, a BGP device sends Update messages to its peers, which must then re-process routes and modify their routing tables. Frequent route flapping consumes extensive bandwidth and CPU resources and can even affect network operations.

Route dampening can address this problem. In most cases, BGP is deployed on complex networks where routes change frequently. Route dampening can be deployed to suppress unstable routes.

BGP dampening measures route stability using a penalty value. The greater the penalty value, the less stable the route. Each time route flapping occurs, BGP adds a penalty value to the involved route (If a route changes from active to inactive, the penalty value increases by 1000. If a route is updated while it is active, the penalty value increases by 500). When the penalty value of a route exceeds the Suppress value, the route is suppressed and will not be added to the IP routing table, and no Update message is advertised to notify BGP peers of the change in the route.

The penalty value of a suppressed route reduces by half following a specific half-life period. When the penalty value decreases to the Reuse value, the route becomes reusable, and BGP adds the route to the IP routing table and advertises an Update message carrying the route to BGP peers. The penalty value, suppression threshold, and half-life are all configurable. Figure 1 shows the process of BGP route dampening.

Route dampening applies only to EBGP routes. IBGP routes cannot be dampened as many IBGP routes are originated from the local AS. This requires the forwarding entries to be the same on IBGP peers in the AS. As such, if IBGP routes are dampened, dampening parameters may vary on different BGP devices, and this can lead to inconsistent forwarding tables.
