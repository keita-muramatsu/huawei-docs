Understanding Import of External Routes to IS-IS

After IS-IS is configured to advertise a default route on an ABR, all traffic destined for outside the local area is forwarded through the ABR because other devices in the area do not have the routes destined for outside the area. As a result, this ABR may be overloaded. In addition, if multiple ABRs are deployed in an area, the selected routes to other areas may not be optimal. To address these issues, configure the ABRs to import external routes so that all other devices in the local area can learn all, or some, of the external routes from the ABRs.

If the other devices in the local area require only some of the external routes, you can configure an export policy for the ABRs to advertise only the required external routes.

IS-IS and other dynamic routing protocols such as OSPF and BGP often import routes from each other. If no routing policy is configured or a routing policy is incorrectly configured on a device where IS-IS, OSPF, and BGP import routes from each other, a Layer 3 routing loop may occur due to a route selection result change. As a result, services are compromised. For details about the cause of the routing loop, see Understanding Routing Loop Detection for Routes Imported to IS-IS.
