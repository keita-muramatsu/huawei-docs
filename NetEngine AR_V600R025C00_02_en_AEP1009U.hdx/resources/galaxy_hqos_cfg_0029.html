HQoS Hierarchical CAR

Scenario Model

On the SD-WAN EVPN network shown in Figure 1, when CPE2 and CPE3 send traffic to CPE1, the traffic rate may exceed the bandwidth of CPE1, causing it to discard packets at any time. To ensure the experience of key VPN users and key services of each VPN user on CPE2 and CPE3, hierarchical CAR can be used to implement multi-level traffic policing in the inbound direction on the WAN side of CPE1:

  • Limit the traffic rate in the inbound direction of CPE1.
  • Allocate bandwidth to each VPN in the inbound direction of CPE1.
  • Allocate bandwidth to services of each VPN in the inbound direction of CPE1.
Figure 1 Network diagram of the hierarchical CAR scenario model

Configuration Model

Hierarchical CAR supports the parent and child traffic policy configuration model. Bandwidth allocation parameters are defined for users and services in the parent traffic policy and child traffic policy, respectively. The child traffic policy is defined in a traffic behavior of the parent traffic policy to associate users with services. Figure 2 shows the configuration model.

Figure 2 Parent and child traffic policy configuration model

Traffic Model

In Figure 3, after packets arrive at a WAN-side physical inbound interface, they enter an SD-WAN tunnel interface. After decapsulating the packets, the tunnel interface performs the HQoS policy for hierarchical CAR on the packets. Specifically, it first performs the L3 user service CAR of the child traffic policy, then the L2 user CAR of the parent traffic policy, and finally the L1 interface CAR.

Figure 3 Network diagram of the hierarchical CAR traffic model

Bandwidth Allocation Model

As shown in Figure 1, after hierarchical CAR is applied to interface 1 in the inbound direction on the WAN side of CPE1, the incoming rate of interface 1 is limited to 100 Mbit/s. The parent traffic policy allocates bandwidth to each VPN based on the bandwidth (100 Mbit/s) on interface 1. The child traffic policy then allocates bandwidth to different services of each VPN based on the bandwidth allocated to each VPN. Figure 4 shows the final bandwidth allocation model.

Figure 4 Bandwidth allocation model

The allocated CIR and PIR values in the preceding figure are for reference only. In practice, the bandwidth allocation scheme needs to be planned according to the distribution of network traffic.

Process of Matching Packets Against the Parent Traffic Policy and Child Traffic Policy

After a traffic policy containing a parent traffic policy and a child traffic policy configured for hierarchical CAR is applied to an inbound interface of the device, the system matches the packets to be forwarded on the interface against rules in the parent traffic policy and child traffic policy, according to which it then determines the subsequent actions for the packets.
